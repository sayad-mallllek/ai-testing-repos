# Hiking boots fit and care

Try hiking boots with the socks you plan to wear on the trail. Leave a little toe room so your toes do not strike the front when descending. A secure heel reduces rubbing; persistent heel lifting or pressure points suggest the fit needs adjustment. Try both boots and walk indoors before deciding. Waterproof hiking boots suit wet or muddy trails but can feel warmer than breathable footwear in dry summer weather. Lightweight boots are useful when reducing carried weight matters. Check product descriptions for whether a particular boot is waterproof; do not infer it from the name alone.

After a hike, remove mud with a soft brush and allow boots to dry naturally away from direct heaters. Remove insoles to help drying. Follow the material care instructions before applying cleaners or waterproofing treatments. No boot guarantees you will avoid blisters. Break in new footwear with shorter walks before a long trip, and carry suitable socks. Boots worn outdoors do not qualify as unused under the store's return policy.
